## 2. Do These Before You Fly

Ordered by deadline. The first item is genuinely urgent and easy to miss.

### Verify Sianna has her own purchased seat on the August 17 return flight

US airlines permit lap-infant travel only for children **under 2 years old**. Sianna turns 2 on **August 15**, which puts her on either side of that line during this trip:

- **Outbound, Thursday Aug 13** — she is still 1. A lap-infant booking is fine.
- **Return, Monday Aug 17** — she is 2. Southwest requires her to occupy **her own purchased seat.**

If she was booked as a lap infant for the round trip, the return leg is non-compliant and you could be stopped at the gate in Orlando at 6:30 AM on a travel morning. That is the worst possible time to discover it.

**Action:** call Southwest at **1-800-435-9792**, confirm the Aug 17 booking has a paid seat for Sianna, and add one if it doesn't. Do this today — fares only go up, and the flight may fill.

While you're at it: if she has her own seat, bring an **FAA-approved car seat** for the plane. It's safer than a lap belt for a 2-year-old, she's far more likely to nap in a familiar seat, and you need a car seat in Orlando anyway (see the departure-day note in section 3).

### Book dining now

The 60-day reservation window is already open for all of your dates, so book today. Use the My Disney Experience app or call **(407) 939-3463** (407-WDW-DINE).

1. **Tusker House Restaurant**, Animal Kingdom — character breakfast with Mickey, Donald, Daisy, and Goofy in safari outfits. Target **8:00–9:00 AM on Fri Aug 14**. Roughly $52/adult.
2. **The Crystal Palace**, Magic Kingdom — character buffet with Winnie the Pooh, Tigger, Piglet, and Eeyore. Target **11:00 AM–12:30 PM on Sat Aug 15**. Breakfast $54/adult, lunch and dinner $64/adult.

### On Lightning Lane: buy it for Magic Kingdom, skip it for Animal Kingdom

Lightning Lane Multi Pass runs about **$19–35 per person at Animal Kingdom** and **$32–45 per person at Magic Kingdom**, priced dynamically. Off-site guests book 3 days out; Disney Resort guests get 7 days, which means the best Animal Kingdom slots are partly picked over before your window even opens.

- **Animal Kingdom: skip it.** The only attractions there with punishing lines are Avatar Flight of Passage (44") and Expedition Everest (44"), and nobody in your party is riding those with a 2-year-old along. Kilimanjaro Safaris and Na'vi River Journey are very manageable if you're there at opening.
- **Magic Kingdom: worth it** (roughly $100–135 for three adults). Peter Pan's Flight, The Many Adventures of Winnie the Pooh, Under the Sea, Jungle Cruise, and the carousel-adjacent Fantasyland rides all build long standby lines, and they're all rides Sianna can do. A 2-year-old's patience is the scarcest resource on this trip, and this is the only thing money can buy that directly protects it.

### One booking caution

Mid-August is peak Atlantic hurricane season. Orlando's odds of a direct hit in any given week are low, but check your airline's and hotel's weather-waiver policies now so you know your options if a storm develops.

**Be honest with yourself about the 10:00 PM fireworks.** That is roughly three hours past a normal 2-year-old bedtime, at the end of a very long day, and the show is loud. She will not remember it. Three ways to play it, in the order I'd recommend them:

1. **Watch from the beach or lawn at Disney's Contemporary Resort.** No crowd, no ticket needed, a lovely view straight across Seven Seas Lagoon, room for her to lie in the stroller, and your ride home is right there. You lose the castle projections and gain everything else. With a 2-year-old, this is the smart version.
2. **Watch from inside the park, but don't fight for the hub.** The castle-forward hub is a crush by 9:00 PM, strollers get cleared out of it, and you'll be pinned there afterward. Stand further back near Casey's Corner or in the Plaza so you can walk out the second it ends.

### A note on characters at age 2

This is the one thing most first-time toddler trips get wrong. Plenty of 2-year-olds who adore Mickey on television are frightened by a silent, six-foot, full-costume Mickey at close range. It's completely normal and it isn't a failure.

- **Don't force it.** Let her watch other children go first from the safety of your arms. Disney's character performers are trained to read this instantly — they'll kneel, slow down, wave from a distance, or offer a fist bump instead of a hug.
- **"Face characters" are easier.** The princesses, Mirabel, Aladdin and Jasmine, and Belle all have visible human faces and can talk to her. Many toddlers who panic at Pooh are delighted by Cinderella.
- **Character meals are the gentlest possible introduction**, which is a large part of why Tusker House and Crystal Palace are the backbone of this plan. She stays in her own chair, on her own terms, and the character leaves after 30 seconds.
- **Take the photo anyway.** A shot of her burying her face in your shoulder while Tigger waves is a better family photograph in ten years than a posed one.

## 4. What Sianna Can Actually Ride

Good news: almost everything a 2-year-old wants to do has no height requirement. A typical 2-year-old is around **33 to 35 inches**, so realistically she clears none of the height-gated rides except possibly Tomorrowland Speedway. Measure her against a wall before you go — height is checked with shoes on at the entrance and re-checked at boarding.

### How Rider Switch works

With three adults and one toddler, this is how the grown-ups still ride the big stuff. Bring your **whole group, including Sianna**, to the attraction entrance and tell the cast member you want Rider Switch. They'll split you into Party A (rides first, via the standby queue) and Party B (waits outside with Sianna — a maximum of two people ride on the second turn). They scan Party B's tickets and tell you where to return. When Party A finishes, Party B enters through the Lightning Lane return without waiting again. If Party B is only one person, someone from Party A can re-ride with them.

The cast member must physically see the child who isn't riding, so don't send one person ahead to set it up.

## 6. Getting Around

### The hotel shuttle: useful for exactly one thing

Your hotel runs a free scheduled shuttle (operated by Platinum Transportation). The most recently published schedule looks like this — **call (407) 239-1144 to confirm before you rely on it**, since the version online dates from May 2025:

**Departing the hotel:** 7:20 AM Magic Kingdom (drops at the TTC) · 8:45 AM EPCOT · 10:40 AM EPCOT · 11:35 AM Hollywood Studios · 5:15 PM Disney Springs

**Returning:** 4:15 PM and 7:50 PM Hollywood Studios · 10:15 PM and 10:30 PM EPCOT · 11:30 PM Disney Springs

Three things this tells you:

1. **The 7:20 AM Magic Kingdom run is excellent** — take it on Aug 15.
2. **There is no direct Animal Kingdom departure and no Magic Kingdom return.** The workaround is to ride to the TTC or EPCOT and transfer to Disney's own buses and monorails, which are free and continuous but add 30–60 minutes each way. On Aug 14, and on the night of Aug 15, just take an Uber.
3. **Only single umbrella-style strollers are allowed on the shuttle.** No wide strollers, no doubles, no wagons.

### On strollers, since she'll live in one

At 2 she'll be in the stroller most of every day, so this decision matters more than it looks. You want something that is **narrow enough for the hotel shuttle** (single umbrella style), **reclines far enough for real in-park napping**, and has **a deep sun canopy**. A stroller she'll actually sleep in is worth more to this trip than Lightning Lane. Add a clip-on fan and a rain cover.

Disney rents strollers in-park for about $15/day, but they don't recline and you can't take them out of the park, so they're a poor fit here.

### What to expect from rideshare

Roughly $15–25 each way between the hotel and any Disney park, and $40–55 to or from MCO. Two specifics:

- **For Magic Kingdom, rideshare drops at the TTC**, not the park gate, so you still need the monorail or ferry. Getting dropped at **Disney's Contemporary Resort** instead lets you walk straight in, and it's the fastest way both in and out.
- **Bring your own car seat.** Florida requires a restraint at this age and drivers don't carry them.

### Not eligible: Early Theme Park Entry

Disney Resort hotels and about 15 select partner hotels get into the parks 30 minutes early. **Embassy Suites Orlando Lake Buena Vista is not on that list**, despite being a Disney Good Neighbor hotel and despite several nearby Hilton properties qualifying. Plan on regular opening times. This is also why your Lightning Lane window is 3 days out rather than 7.

## 7. Surviving August in Orlando With a 2-Year-Old

Mid-August is the hottest, wettest part of the Orlando year: **average highs of 91–92°F, lows around 75°F, humidity near 74%**, and a heat index that regularly clears 100°F. There are thunderstorms on about **18 days of the month**, almost always in the afternoon, and roughly a 60–67% chance of a wet day on any given date. Sunrise is around 6:55 AM and sunset around **8:05 PM**.

That climate dictates the entire shape of these plans:

- **Front-load every day.** Everything you actually care about happens between park opening and noon.
- **Treat the nap as fixed, not optional.** From roughly 1:00 to 5:00 PM, be at the pool or asleep. This isn't laziness; at 2 it's the difference between a good trip and a series of meltdowns.
- **Storms usually pass in 30–45 minutes.** Pack ponchos, not umbrellas — umbrellas are useless in crowds and Disney marks ponchos up heavily. Keep an indoor backup ready for every afternoon: Hall of Presidents, Mickey's PhilharMagic, Enchanted Tiki Room, and the Laugh Floor are all air-conditioned and near one another.
- **Lightning stops outdoor entertainment.** Parades and fireworks can be cancelled with no notice.
- **Watch her, not the thermometer.** Toddlers overheat faster than adults and can't tell you they're doing it. Flushed cheeks, unusual quiet, or refusing a drink all mean get inside now.

### Pack list

- **Compact single umbrella stroller that reclines** (required for the hotel shuttle) plus a clip-on fan and a rain cover
- **Toddler ear defenders** — non-negotiable if you're doing the 10:00 PM fireworks
- Ponchos for all four
- **Swim diapers, water shoes, and two full changes of clothes** for Casey Jr. Splash 'N' Soak
- Refillable water bottles, cooling towels, a misting spray bottle
- Sunscreen, wide sun hat, UPF long-sleeve shirt — she'll get more sun in a stroller than you'd think
- Diapers, wipes, and her usual snacks from the Publix run
- **Portable phone battery.** The My Disney Experience app annihilates a phone battery, and your tickets, Lightning Lane, and mobile orders all live inside it
- **Travel car seat** for rideshares and the plane
- A birthday outfit or Minnie ears — visible birthday cues get reactions all day
- Her comfort object. Whatever it is, bring two if you can

### Baby Care Centers

Both parks have free air-conditioned Baby Care Centers with changing tables, nursing areas, a microwave, and basic supplies for sale. Magic Kingdom's is at the top of Main Street, U.S.A. near First Aid; Animal Kingdom's is on Discovery Island. Confirm exact locations in the app. These are the two best places in either park to reset a hot, overtired 2-year-old, and knowing where they are before you need one is worth a lot.

## 8. What's Closed or Changed in August 2026

**Magic Kingdom**

- **Walt Disney's Carousel of Progress** — closed since July 6, 2026, reopening 2027. A real loss, since it was a 21-minute air-conditioned sit-down.
- **Walt Disney World Railroad** — running as a shuttle between the Main Street and Fantasyland stations only. Frontierland station is closed.
- **Rivers of America, Tom Sawyer Island, and the Liberty Belle Riverboat** — permanently gone as of July 2025.
- **Villains Land and Piston Peak (Cars)** — the largest expansion in Magic Kingdom history is under construction behind Big Thunder Mountain and Haunted Mansion. Expect construction walls in Frontierland; neither land opens for years.
- **Pete's Silly Sideshow** (closed January 2026) and **Big Top Souvenirs** (closed February 2026) in Storybook Circus. Pete's is where Minnie, Donald, Daisy, and Goofy normally meet, so that meet-and-greet may be unavailable — check the app, since some guides still list it.
- **The Diamond Horseshoe**, **Frontierland Shootin' Arcade**, **Big Al**, and **Westward Ho** are all closed.
- Good news: **Big Thunder Mountain reopened** May 2026, and **Buzz Lightyear's Space Ranger Spin reopened** April 2026.

**Animal Kingdom**

- **All of DinoLand U.S.A. closed permanently on February 2, 2026** — DINOSAUR, TriceraTop Spin, The Boneyard, Restaurantosaurus, Chester & Hester's, Donald's Dino Bash. It becomes Tropical Americas (an Encanto dark ride, an Indiana Jones ride, and a carousel) in 2027. The park has noticeably fewer rides than usual right now, and losing The Boneyard hurts most for your age group.
- **It's Tough to Be a Bug** was replaced by **Zootopia: Better Zoogether!** in November 2025.
- **New:** **Bluey's Wild World** at Conservation Station opened May 26, 2026. It launched with a virtual queue, but **that ended June 2, 2026** — it is now ordinary walk-up standby, reached via the Wildlife Express Train, operating 8:30 AM to 3:45 PM.
