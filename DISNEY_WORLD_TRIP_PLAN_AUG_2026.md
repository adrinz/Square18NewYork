## 2. Do These Before You Fly

Ordered by deadline. The first item is genuinely urgent.

### Confirm whether Sianna needs a park ticket (do this first)

Disney requires a theme park ticket for every guest **age 3 and older**. Children under 3 get in free.

If Sianna turns 3 on **August 15**, she needs a ticket for that day. There is one exception: guests on a *Walt Disney Travel Company vacation package* (a Disney Resort hotel and tickets booked in a single Disney transaction) have their child's age locked to their age at check-in. **That exception almost certainly does not apply to you** — Embassy Suites is a Disney Good Neighbor hotel, not a Disney Resort hotel, and your itinerary shows the hotel and the tickets as separate confirmations.

Your ticket record also lists exactly three guests, all "Ages 10+," so no child ticket was purchased.

**Action:** call Disney at **(407) 939-5277** and confirm. If they confirm she needs one, buy a 1-day Magic Kingdom ticket for Aug 15 (budget roughly $120–190 with tax for a peak August Saturday). ### On Lightning Lane: buy it for Magic Kingdom, skip it for Animal Kingdom

Lightning Lane Multi Pass runs about **$19–35 per person at Animal Kingdom** and **$32–45 per person at Magic Kingdom**, priced dynamically. Off-site guests book 3 days out; Disney Resort guests get 7 days, which means the best Animal Kingdom slots are partly picked over before your window even opens.

- **Animal Kingdom: skip it.** The attractions with real lines there are Avatar Flight of Passage (44") and Expedition Everest (44"), and Sianna can't ride either. Kilimanjaro Safaris and Na'vi River Journey are very manageable if you're there at opening.
- **Magic Kingdom: worth it** (roughly $100–135 for three adults). Peter Pan's Flight, The Many Adventures of Winnie the Pooh, Under the Sea, Jungle Cruise, and Haunted Mansion all build long standby lines, and they're all rides Sianna can do. On a birthday, buying back two hours of standing in the sun is a good trade.

Sianna doesn't need her own Lightning Lane if she doesn't have a ticket — she just comes along.

### One booking caution

Mid-August is peak Atlantic hurricane season. Orlando's odds of a direct hit in any given week are low, but check your airline's and hotel's weather-waiver policies now so you know your options if a storm develops.

**Fireworks with a toddler — three specifics that matter:**

1. **Bring ear defenders.** The finale is genuinely loud and the concussive booms are what frighten small children, not the lights. This is the highest-value $20 in your bag.

**Getting out after fireworks:** the monorail and ferry lines after a 10:00 PM show are brutal. Best move with a stroller is to **walk to the Contemporary Resort** (about 10 minutes on a flat, well-lit path) and get an Uber from that porte-cochère. Fair warning: it takes up to an hour of sitting still, which is a lot to ask of a brand-new 3-year-old, and the boutique doesn't stock shoes small enough for her.

### How Rider Switch works

With three adults and one toddler, this is how you all still ride the big stuff. Bring your **whole group, including Sianna**, to the attraction entrance and tell the cast member you want Rider Switch. They'll split you into Party A (rides first, in the standby queue) and Party B (waits outside with Sianna — maximum two people ride on the second turn). They scan Party B's tickets and tell you where to return. When Party A is done, Party B enters through the Lightning Lane return without waiting again. If Party B is only one person, someone from Party A can re-ride with them.

The cast member must physically see the child who isn't riding, so don't send one person ahead to set it up.

## 6. Getting Around

### The hotel shuttle: useful for exactly one thing

Your hotel runs a free scheduled shuttle (operated by Platinum Transportation). The most recently published schedule looks like this — **call (407) 239-1144 to confirm before you rely on it**, since the version online dates from May 2025:

**Departing the hotel:** 7:20 AM Magic Kingdom (drops at the TTC) · 8:45 AM EPCOT · 10:40 AM EPCOT · 11:35 AM Hollywood Studios · 5:15 PM Disney Springs

**Returning:** 4:15 PM and 7:50 PM Hollywood Studios · 10:15 PM and 10:30 PM EPCOT · 11:30 PM Disney Springs

Three things this tells you:

1. **The 7:20 AM Magic Kingdom run is excellent** — take it on Aug 15.
2. **There is no direct Animal Kingdom departure and no Magic Kingdom return.** The workaround is to ride to the TTC or EPCOT and transfer to Disney's own buses and monorails, which are free and run continuously but add 30–60 minutes each way. On Aug 14 and on the night of Aug 15, just take an Uber.
3. **Only single umbrella-style strollers are allowed on the shuttle.** No wide strollers, no doubles, no wagons. This alone may decide which stroller you pack.

### What to expect from rideshare

Roughly $15–25 each way between the hotel and any Disney park, and $40–55 to or from MCO. Two useful specifics:

- **For Magic Kingdom, rideshare drops at the TTC**, not the park gate, so you still need the monorail or ferry. Getting dropped at **Disney's Contemporary Resort** instead lets you walk straight in, and it's the fastest way both in and out.
- Bring your own car seat (see the departure-day note above).

### Not eligible: Early Theme Park Entry

Disney Resort hotels and about 15 select partner hotels get into the parks 30 minutes early. **Embassy Suites Orlando Lake Buena Vista is not on that list**, despite being a Disney Good Neighbor hotel and despite several nearby Hilton properties qualifying. Plan on regular opening times, and note this also means your Lightning Lane window is 3 days out rather than 7.

## 7. Surviving August in Orlando With a Toddler

Mid-August is the hottest, wettest part of the Orlando year: **average highs of 91–92°F, lows around 75°F, humidity near 74%**, and a heat index that regularly clears 100°F. There are thunderstorms on about **18 days of the month**, almost always in the afternoon, and about a 60–67% chance of a wet day on any given date. Sunrise is around 6:55 AM and sunset around **8:05 PM**.

That climate dictates the whole shape of these plans:

- **Front-load the day.** Everything you actually care about happens between park opening and noon.
- **Take the midday break seriously.** From roughly 1:00 to 5:00 PM, be at the pool or asleep. This isn't laziness; it's the difference between a good trip and a series of meltdowns.
- **Storms usually pass in 30–45 minutes.** Pack ponchos, not umbrellas — umbrellas are useless in crowds and Disney sells ponchos at a heavy markup. Have an indoor backup queued up for every afternoon: Hall of Presidents, Mickey's PhilharMagic, Enchanted Tiki Room, and the Laugh Floor are all air-conditioned and near each other.
- **Lightning stops outdoor entertainment.** Parades and fireworks can be cancelled with no notice.

### Pack list, toddler-specific

- **Compact single umbrella stroller** (required for the hotel shuttle) plus a clip-on fan and a rain cover
- **Toddler ear defenders** — non-negotiable for the 10:00 PM fireworks
- Ponchos for all four
- **Swim diapers, water shoes, and two full changes of clothes** for Casey Jr. Splash 'N' Soak
- Refillable water bottles, cooling towels, a misting spray bottle
- Sunscreen, wide sun hat, UPF long-sleeve shirt — she'll get more sun than you think in a stroller
- Snacks from that Publix run
- **Portable phone battery.** The My Disney Experience app annihilates a phone battery, and your tickets, Lightning Lane, and mobile orders all live in it
- **Travel car seat** for rideshares
- A birthday outfit or Minnie ears — visible birthday cues get reactions all day
- A photo of her birth certificate on your phone

### Baby Care Centers

Both parks have free air-conditioned Baby Care Centers with changing tables, nursing areas, a microwave, and basic supplies for sale. Magic Kingdom's is at the top of Main Street, U.S.A. near First Aid; Animal Kingdom's is on Discovery Island. Confirm exact locations in the app — these are the best places in either park to reset a hot, overtired toddler.

## 8. What's Closed or Changed in August 2026

Worth knowing so nobody is disappointed at the map.

**Magic Kingdom**

- **Walt Disney's Carousel of Progress** — closed since July 6, 2026, reopening in 2027. A loss, since it was a 21-minute air-conditioned sit-down.
- **Walt Disney World Railroad** — running as a shuttle between the Main Street and Fantasyland stations only. Frontierland station is closed.
- **Rivers of America, Tom Sawyer Island, and the Liberty Belle Riverboat** — permanently gone as of July 2025.
- **Villains Land and Piston Peak (Cars)** — the largest expansion in Magic Kingdom history is under construction behind Big Thunder Mountain and Haunted Mansion. Expect construction walls in Frontierland; neither land opens for years.
- **Pete's Silly Sideshow** (closed January 2026) and **Big Top Souvenirs** (closed February 2026) in Storybook Circus. Pete's is where Minnie, Donald, Daisy, and Goofy normally meet, so that meet-and-greet may be unavailable — check the app, since some guides still list it.
- **The Diamond Horseshoe**, **Frontierland Shootin' Arcade**, **Big Al**, and **Westward Ho** are all closed.
- Good news: **Big Thunder Mountain reopened** May 2026, and **Buzz Lightyear's Space Ranger Spin reopened** April 2026.

**Animal Kingdom**

- **All of DinoLand U.S.A. closed permanently on February 2, 2026** — DINOSAUR, TriceraTop Spin, The Boneyard, Restaurantosaurus, Chester & Hester's, Donald's Dino Bash. It becomes Tropical Americas (an Encanto dark ride, an Indiana Jones ride, and a carousel) in 2027. The park has noticeably fewer rides than usual right now, and losing The Boneyard hurts most for your age group.
- **It's Tough to Be a Bug** was replaced by **Zootopia: Better Zoogether!** in November 2025.
- **New:** **Bluey's Wild World** at Conservation Station opened May 26, 2026. The virtual queue now also gates the Wildlife Express Train and the Conservation Station animal-care experiences.
